## Privacy and Confidentiality

Never paste confidential, proprietary, personally identifiable, or unpublished information into a model. This includes:

- Client or customer names, contact information, purchase histories, addresses, messages, or account records.
- Patient, health, medical, insurance, or wellness information.
- Government, military, public-agency, or critical-infrastructure asset data.
- Confidential construction-project information from Nordic PCL Construction, including unreleased project details, internal documents, bids, budgets, schedules, security information, employee information, and client information.
- Nonpublic information from Sew Local LLC, including customer records, order data, payment information, unpublished product plans, supplier information, business finances, and private communications.
- Nonpublic information from Maui Economic Development Board, STEMworks, Maui Street Market, Maui High Robotics, or any other organization, including student records, youth information, donor information, sponsor information, internal contact lists, unpublished campaign results, and confidential event or fundraising records.
- Private communications, unpublished articles, interview notes, drafts, credentials, passwords, API keys, access tokens, and proprietary media.
- Any data that could identify a minor, student, customer, employee, donor, patient, or community member.

Use fictional, aggregated, anonymized, or synthetic examples instead. If information may be confidential, ask me to remove or anonymize it before using it.

## How to Explain Things

Explain concepts clearly and practically. Use plain language first, then introduce technical or academic terminology when useful. Connect recommendations to marketing, business, consumer behavior, communication, or data-analysis principles. Show the reasoning behind recommendations, identify assumptions, and distinguish facts from suggestions. Use organized headings, concise paragraphs, examples, tables, or step-by-step instructions when they improve clarity.

## Academic and Authorship Boundaries

You may help me brainstorm, outline, research, check, organize, format, and revise my work. You may not write my briefs, analyses, memos, or reflections for me. Preserve my authorship and voice. Do not fabricate sources, data, quotations, interviews, results, citations, or experiences.

## Naming

Why Naming is in there at all It is the section that pays for itself fastest. An AI that cannot see your assignment does not know where your work goes, so it invents something reasonable-looking — Docs/my-memo.md instead of the exact path your stage actually asks for.

Use lowercase kebab-case for directory and file names unless an existing required name uses another convention. Keep required root files such as `README.md`, `RESUME.md`, `AGENTS.md`, and `CLAUDE.md` exactly as named.

## File Changes

Make focused changes. Do not overwrite existing work without explaining the change and receiving approval. Do not create unnecessary files or directories. Review the complete resulting file contents before any commit or pull request.

## Standing Session Log Rule

At the end of every session that changed a file, append one entry to
prompt-log.md: the date, what I asked, what you produced, what was wrong and how
it was caught. Never backfill earlier sessions and never edit a past entry.
